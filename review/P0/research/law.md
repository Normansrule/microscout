# Wisp: legal rules for recreational flight in San Pedro, CA

Research date: 2026-10-26. This report states the rules. It is not legal advice.

How to read the tags:
- **[PRIMARY]** means the text was read on a government source: faa.gov, ecfr.gov, govinfo.gov, a city, county or state agency, or NPS.
- **[MIRROR]** means the regulation text came from a non-government copy of the CFR (Gleim, federal-regs.com), because the eCFR section page was not reachable from this session.
- **[SECONDARY]** means a news, law-firm or forum source.
- **UNCONFIRMED** means it could not be checked against a primary source in this session.

## 0. Headline findings

1. **The registration threshold is 0.55 lb, which is about 249.5 g, not 250 g.** The regulation exempts a recreational aircraft that "weighs 0.55 pounds or less on takeoff, including everything that is on board or otherwise attached" (14 CFR 48.15(b)). 0.55 lb works out to 249.48 g. AC 91-57D and the AIM say "more than 0.55 pounds (250 grams)". The FAA recreational page says "250 grams (0.55 lbs) or more". These wordings are close but not identical. **The safe target is ≤ 249 g all-up, measured ready to fly.**
2. **Under 0.55 lb and flown strictly recreationally under 49 U.S.C. 44809:**
   - No registration is required.
   - No marking is required.
   - No Remote ID is required: Part 89 applies only to aircraft "registered or required to be registered".
   - Do not register voluntarily. Once the drone is "registered", Remote ID applies.
   - TRUST, a community-based organization's (CBO) safety guidelines, visual line of sight (VLOS), the 400 ft limit in Class G, and LAANC in controlled airspace all still apply.
3. **There is no speed limit for recreational flyers in the statute, the regulations, or AC 91-57D.** Part 107's 87 kt / 100 mph limit (107.51(a)) does not apply, because Part 107 "does not apply to … Any aircraft subject to the provisions of 49 U.S.C. 44809" (107.1(b)(2)). VLOS, giving way to manned aircraft, and the ban on careless or reckless operation still limit how fast the drone can be flown in practice.
4. **A monetized vlog is not recreational flying.** AC 91-57D says: "Recreational purposes may not include activities such as flights for any compensation, monetary or otherwise". If the flight is commercial, it falls under Part 107, and the sub-250 g exemption from registration does not apply.
5. **City of Los Angeles land, which covers most of San Pedro's public open space, is close to a no-fly area for hobbyists:**
   - LAMC 63.44 B.8 bans flying aircraft or powered models in city parks "except in areas specifically set aside".
   - Port Police say that within Harbor Department property "there are NO areas set aside for UAS operations".
   - LA County beaches (for example White Point / Royal Palms) ban model aircraft (County Code 17.12.425).
6. **National-security drone restrictions apply on Terminal Island:**
   - FCI Terminal Island (San Pedro) is under a 14 CFR 99.7 restriction from the surface to 400 ft, within the facility boundary.
   - USCG Base Los Angeles/Long Beach is also restricted.

## 1. Recreational flying under 49 U.S.C. 44809

### 1.1 Statutory conditions (49 U.S.C. 44809(a)(1)–(8)) [PRIMARY]

Source: govinfo.gov USCODE 2018 edition. The quote limit applied, so the wording below is close to the statute but not verbatim. A person may fly without Part 107 certification only if every one of these is met:

| # | Condition |
|---|---|
| (1) | The aircraft is "flown strictly for recreational purposes". |
| (2) | It is operated under the safety guidelines of a community-based organization (CBO), developed with the FAA. |
| (3) | It stays "within the visual line of sight of the person operating the aircraft or a visual observer co-located and in direct communication with the operator". |
| (4) | It does not interfere with manned aircraft and "gives way to any manned aircraft". |
| (5) | In Class B, C or D airspace, or the surface area of Class E for an airport, prior authorization from the Administrator is required. |
| (6) | In Class G airspace, it is flown "from the surface to not more than 400 feet above ground level". |
| (7) | The operator has passed an aeronautical knowledge and safety test (TRUST) and keeps proof of passage. |
| (8) | The aircraft is registered and marked under chapter 441, where required, with proof available on request. |

Other parts of the statute:
- **44809(b):** an operation that does not meet (a) must comply with the general UAS rules (in practice, Part 107).
- **44809(e):** the FAA can still enforce against anyone who endangers the national airspace system.
- **44809(f):** the FAA can still make rules on registration and marking, remote identification and other safety standards. This is the authority for Part 89.

### 1.2 FAA recreational flyer page (faa.gov/uas/recreational_flyers, last updated 2026-10-14) [PRIMARY]

The page lists 10 rules, including:
- "Follow the safety guidelines of an FAA-recognized Community-Based Organization (CBO)."
- "Keep your drone within the visual line of sight or use a visual observer who is co-located (physically next to) and in direct communication with you."
- "Fly at or below FAA-authorized altitudes in controlled airspace … only with prior FAA authorization by using LAANC or DroneZone."
- "Fly at or below 400 feet in Class G (uncontrolled) airspace."
- "Take The Recreational UAS Safety Test (TRUST) and carry proof of test passage when flying."
- "Have a current FAA registration, mark (PDF) your drones on the outside with the registration number … carry proof of registration with you when flying." (This applies only when registration is required.)
- "Do not operate your drone in a manner that endangers the safety of the national airspace system."

On weight and Remote ID, the page says:
- "You must register if your drone weighs 250 grams (0.55 lbs) or more."
- "if your drone requires an FAA registration number it will also be required to broadcast Remote ID information (unless flown within a Federally-Recognized Identification Area, or FRIA)."
- TRUST is free and online.

### 1.3 FAA-recognized CBOs (page last updated 2026-05-05) [PRIMARY]

- "Recreational drone flyers must use FAA-recognized CBO safety guidelines."
- "recreational flyers are not required to become members of a CBO."
- The recognized CBOs are:
  - Academy of Model Aeronautics (AMA)
  - First Person View Freedom Coalition
  - Flite Test Community Association
  - STEM+C Inc.

### 1.4 AC 91-57D replaces AC 91-57C [PRIMARY]

- **AC 91-57D is dated 6/24/25.** ¶1.4 says: "AC 91-57C, Exception for Limited Recreational Operations of Unmanned Aircraft, dated October 20, 2022, is canceled." Use 91-57D, not 91-57C.
- The AC says of itself: "This is a guidance document. Its content is not legally binding in its own right."
- On registration, ¶2.2.8.1 says: "Recreational flyers must register UA weighing more than 0.55 pounds (250 grams) at takeoff."
- On Remote ID, ¶1.7.4 says: "Recreational operators must comply with 14 CFR Part 89". A FRIA "is a location where UA may operate without remote identification."
- On what counts as recreational, ¶2.2.1.1 says:
  - "Recreational purposes may not include activities such as flights for any compensation, monetary or otherwise."
  - "A person may not combine recreational and commercial purposes in a single flight."
  - The answer is "situation-specific and evaluated on a case-by-case basis."
  - The AC does not mention YouTube or social-media monetization specifically.

### 1.5 Registration: is a sub-250 g recreational drone exempt? Yes, if it is ≤ 0.55 lb.

**14 CFR 48.15** [MIRROR: Gleim and federal-regs.com, "Updated January 1, 2026"; not read on ecfr.gov]:
- Lead-in: "No person may operate a small unmanned aircraft that is eligible for registration under 49 U.S.C. 44101-44103" unless one of the following applies:
  - (a) "The owner has registered and marked the aircraft in accordance with this part;"
  - (b) "The aircraft is operated exclusively in compliance with 49 U.S.C. 44809 and weighs 0.55 pounds or less on takeoff, including everything that is on board or otherwise attached to the aircraft"; or
  - (c) the aircraft is an Armed Forces aircraft.

The **2015 registration rule preamble** (80 FR 78594, govinfo) [PRIMARY]:
- It says takeoff weight includes payload: "the manner in which that mass is achieved is irrelevant."
- Its summary table for 48.15 reads "more than 0.55 pounds (250 grams) on takeoff".

**Does the weight include the battery, camera and accessories?** Yes. The limit is takeoff weight "including everything that is on board or otherwise attached". For Wisp that includes:
- the flight battery and any camera (such as a GoPro or an action-camera core);
- mounts, ND filters, prop guards, lights;
- a Remote ID module, tape, and anything else attached.

**What happens if a payload pushes it over 0.55 lb?** The 48.15(b) exemption no longer applies for that flight. The aircraft must then be registered and marked, and Part 89 Remote ID applies, because it is now "required to be registered". It does not matter that the bare airframe is under the limit. The test is applied each time the aircraft takes off.

### 1.6 Remote ID (14 CFR Part 89; eCFR up to date as of 10/22/2026) [PRIMARY]

- **§89.101(a):** the subpart applies to "(1) Persons operating unmanned aircraft registered or required to be registered under part 47 or 48 of this chapter" and to foreign civil UAS.
  - A drone of 0.55 lb or less flown only under 44809 is not "required to be registered", so Part 89 does not apply to it.
  - If the owner registers it voluntarily, it becomes "registered" and Part 89 does apply.
- **§89.105:** after Sept 16, 2023, no person may fly a covered aircraft unless the flight meets §89.110 (standard Remote ID) or §89.115 (alternatives).
- **§89.115(a), broadcast module:** the pilot must make sure the module broadcasts from takeoff to shutdown, and must keep the aircraft in sight "at all times throughout the operation."
- **§89.115(b), FRIA:** the aircraft must "remain within the boundaries of an FAA-recognized identification area throughout the operation".
- **FAA Remote ID page** (last updated 2025-03-19): Remote ID covers "Drones which are required to be registered or are registered, including those flown for recreation". It lists three ways to comply: a standard Remote ID drone, a broadcast module, or flying in a FRIA.

## 2. Speed limit

- **Part 107** (14 CFR 107.51(a)) [PRIMARY via Cornell LII]: "The groundspeed of the small unmanned aircraft may not exceed 87 knots (100 miles per hour)."
- **Part 107 does not apply to recreational flying.** §107.1(b)(2) (eCFR) [PRIMARY]: "This part does not apply to the following: … (2) Any aircraft subject to the provisions of 49 U.S.C. 44809".
- **The statute and AC 91-57D set no speed limit.** Neither text has a speed provision. The AC search returned nothing on speed.
- **CBO guidelines:** the AMA National Model Aircraft Safety Code found (effective 01/01/2018, modelaircraft.org/files/105.PDF) has no speed limit.
  - The other CBOs' current guidelines were not reviewed. Whether any CBO sets a speed limit is UNCONFIRMED.
  - In practice, the CBO the owner chooses to follow may add limits.
- **What still limits speed in practice:**
  - VLOS (44809(a)(3)).
  - Giving way to manned aircraft (a)(4).
  - The FAA's ability to enforce against "careless or reckless operations, or operations endangering persons or property" (AC 91-57D ¶1.7.3).
  - **UNCONFIRMED:** 18 U.S.C. 39B (unsafe operation of unmanned aircraft) was not retrieved.

## 3. Visual line of sight, follow-me, FPV, and flying from a moving vehicle

- **VLOS rule.** The statute requires VLOS of "the person operating the aircraft or a visual observer co-located and in direct communication with the operator." AC 91-57D ¶2.2.3.1 says the operator or visual observer must see the UA "throughout the entire flight to ensure it does not present a collision hazard."
- **Follow-me.** AC 91-57D does not mention follow-me modes.
  - It says operators should "be familiar with the automated features a UA may have, and how the UA would behave when those features are activated" (¶3.3.2.1.1(6)).
  - It also says "the recreational flyer is responsible for the safety of the flight during emergencies" (¶3.3.2.7.2).
  - **Result: during a follow-me flight the owner, or a co-located visual observer, must keep Wisp in direct sight at all times.** A drone trailing behind a walking or riding pilot who is not watching it does not meet that.
- **Corrective lenses only.** The AMA code says: "I will maintain visual contact of an RC model aircraft without enhancement other than corrective lenses prescribed to me."
- **FPV.** AC 91-57D ¶2.2.3.1 says:
  - "The use of a VO is necessary if the recreational flyer wants to use first person view (FPV) devices."
  - "The use of a VO is optional, but a VO enables the recreational flyer to look away from the UA for extended periods."
- **Flying while walking, cycling or driving.**
  - Neither 44809 nor AC 91-57D addresses flying from a moving vehicle. The AC contains neither "moving vehicle" nor "vehicle".
  - Part 107's moving-vehicle rules (107.25, 107.145) do not apply to 44809 flyers.
  - So no federal recreational rule bans walking or cycling while flying. VLOS, giving way, and the ban on careless or reckless operation still apply.
  - Flying while also driving a car is a separate matter of state traffic law, and is **UNCONFIRMED**: California Vehicle Code 23123.5 on handheld devices was not retrieved.
  - The AMA code says: "I will avoid flying directly over unprotected people, moving vehicles, and occupied structures."

## 4. Flying over people, at night, and over moving vehicles

- **Over people.** No federal regulation governs 44809 flights over people. Part 107 Subpart D does not apply.
  - AC 91-57D ¶3.3.2.1.1 asks CBOs to consider "restrictions on operations over people, establishing buffer areas".
  - The AMA code says: "avoid flying directly over unprotected people…", and "I will not fly a powered model outdoors closer than 25 feet to any individual".
  - LAMC 56.31 reportedly bans flying "closer than 25 feet to any individual, except the operator or the operator's helper(s)". **UNCONFIRMED:** this comes from a forum excerpt, and the city clerk PDF of Ordinance 183912 was not reachable.
- **Night.**
  - AC 91-57D ¶3.3.2 says: "an individual could not conduct night operations unless the CBO safety guidelines being followed address night flight."
  - ¶3.3.2.5 says the FAA "strongly recommends" anti-collision lights "that can be seen from 3 statute miles away", and allows flight without lights where the area is "sufficiently illuminated" to keep VLOS.
  - **City of LA: daylight only.** LAMC 56.31 reportedly bans operation "other than during daylight hours defined as between official sunrise and official sunset" (forum excerpt, UNCONFIRMED). LA Port Police Training Bulletin 25-001 [PRIMARY, city agency] states the same daylight-only rule for Harbor Department property.
- **Over moving vehicles.** No federal recreational rule applies. 107.145 is Part 107 only. The AMA code says to avoid flying directly over moving vehicles.

## 6. Local rules

### 6.1 City of Los Angeles parks: LAMC 63.44 B.8

- **Text** [SECONDARY transcription; the section itself was not read on amlegal]: "No person shall land, release, take off or fly any balloon, except children toy balloons not inflated with any flammable material, helicopter, parakite, hang glider, aircraft or powered models thereof, except in areas specifically set aside therefor."
- **Port Police Training Bulletin 25-001, "Enforcement of Unmanned Aircraft Systems"** [PRIMARY, Port of LA]:
  - "Within the Harbor Department properties, there are NO areas set aside for UAS operations."
  - "Absent a Temporary Use Permit (TEUP)," flying on Harbor Department property or waterways violates LAMC 63.44(b)(8).
  - A violation is a misdemeanor. Officers may arrest, and the "UAS and its equipment, including the Micro SD card," are booked as evidence.
  - The bulletin also states the daylight-only and 400 ft limits.
- **Port Police Training Bulletin TB-10-001** [PRIMARY]:
  - LAMC 63.44 B reaches "any park or other City-owned Harbor Department designated and controlled property".
  - **Cabrillo Beach is Port property leased to the City Department of Recreation and Parks.**
- **Point Fermin Park** is a City of LA park, so 63.44 B.8 applies. This is general knowledge, not taken from a source: **UNCONFIRMED**.
- **LAMC 56.31** (City UAS ordinance, Ordinance 183912, effective Dec 2015) [SECONDARY forum excerpts, primary PDF not reachable, **UNCONFIRMED**]. Reported provisions:
  - a misdemeanor penalty;
  - no flight within 5 miles of an airport without control-tower authorization;
  - give way to manned aircraft;
  - daylight only;
  - stay 25 ft from individuals.

### 6.2 LA County

- **Beaches.** County Code 17.12.425 [PRIMARY: quoted in a Board of Supervisors motion dated 2026-04-14]: "[n]o person shall operate any model airplane, boat, helicopter, or similar craft in, on, or over any beach or the Pacific Ocean except in an area that may be established and/or designated for such use by the Director".
  - The motion says the County "currently has a comprehensive prohibition on drone use on County beaches for all purposes."
  - **White Point / Royal Palms Beach is run by LA County Beaches & Harbors** (FilmLA), so the beach ban applies.
- **County parks.** **UNCONFIRMED.** A secondary source cites County Code 17.04.630 and the Parks FAQ: "The Department's policy on drones is the same as for model airplanes and helicopters." It names Santa Fe Dam and Whittier Narrows as the sanctioned model-aviation sites.

### 6.3 California State Parks

- **Angeles District Superintendent's Order No. 915-22-004**, effective 2022-07-01 [PRIMARY], under 14 CCR 4326 and others: "No person shall launch, land or operate a powered unmanned aircraft or unmanned aerial system (UAS)" in any Angeles District park unit. Written exemptions are possible from the Superintendent.
- There are no State Park units in San Pedro itself. Point Fermin, Cabrillo and White Point are city or county land.

### 6.4 Rancho Palos Verdes (rpvca.gov/1319/Drones and the City Drone Policy) [PRIMARY]

- City parks ban "motorized or radio-controlled models" (RPVMC 12.16.040) unless in a designated area or authorized in writing by the City Manager.
- The only drone site is **Point Vicente Park/Civic Center**, with a SCORCH permit. The permit requires:
  - $10 to the City and $20 to the club, each year;
  - AMA membership;
  - FAA registration with the number displayed (the City requires this even for sub-250 g drones);
  - a 400 ft ceiling, and flying only in the designated area.
- "The use of drones is permitted on private property in the City of RPV, as long as the owner of the property consents".
- The Palos Verdes Nature Preserve is not addressed on these pages. **UNCONFIRMED.**

### 6.5 National Park Service [PRIMARY]

- NPS RM-60 (2024), Appendix 8: "All superintendents are required to insert closure language in the park compendium prohibiting launching, landing, or operating unmanned aircraft".
- This covers aircraft "used for any purpose, including for recreation". The FAA 44809 exception does not create an NPS exemption.
- Nearby NPS land includes the Santa Monica Mountains NRA and Channel Islands.

### 6.6 California privacy: Civil Code 1708.8. **UNCONFIRMED**

leginfo was not reachable, so the following is from memory. 1708.8(a) makes a person liable for "physical invasion of privacy" who knowingly enters "onto the land or into the airspace above the land of another person without permission … in order to capture any type of visual image, sound recording, or other physical impression of the plaintiff engaging in a private, personal, or familial activity", where the invasion is "offensive to a reasonable person".
- The airspace wording was added in 2015 (AB 856).
- Penal Code 647(j) (invasive recording) may also apply.
- For a vlog, do not fly low over private yards, and do not record identifiable people in private settings without consent.

## 7. Checklist

### Before the first outdoor flight (Wisp ≤ 0.55 lb, strictly recreational)

1. **Weigh the fully assembled aircraft as it will fly:** battery, camera, mounts, guards, lights. Target ≤ 249 g. 0.55 lb is 249.48 g, so 250 g is already over. Re-weigh after any change.
2. **Take TRUST.** It is free and online. Keep the certificate on you, on paper or your phone (44809(a)(7); AC ¶2.2.7.2).
3. **Choose a CBO and follow its safety guidelines:**
   - AMA
   - First Person View Freedom Coalition
   - Flite Test Community Association
   - STEM+C
   - Membership is not required. Read the code for its limits on night, FPV, distance from people, and speed.
4. **Keep the flight strictly recreational.** If the vlog is monetized or sponsored, the flight is likely commercial: Part 107 then applies, and so do registration and Remote ID.
5. **Do not register voluntarily.** Registering triggers Remote ID.
6. **At every site, check B4UFLY or LAANC:**
   - Class G: stay at or below 400 ft above ground.
   - Controlled airspace: get LAANC from a recreational-approved supplier, at or below the grid ceiling.
   - Check for TFRs, stadium events, and the FCI Terminal Island and USCG Base restrictions.
7. **Check who owns the land:**
   - Not City of LA parks, Port/Harbor property, or Cabrillo Beach, without a set-aside area or permit.
   - Not LA County beaches (White Point / Royal Palms).
   - Not RPV parks, except Point Vicente with a SCORCH permit.
   - Not Angeles District State Parks or NPS units.
   - Private land with the owner's permission is the main remaining option.
8. **Keep VLOS the whole flight, including in follow-me.** Use a co-located visual observer for FPV, or when you need to look away. Give way to all manned aircraft. Avoid flying directly over people and moving vehicles.
9. **Fly in daylight only in the City of LA.** For night flying anywhere, it must be covered by your CBO's guidelines, with lighting visible at 3 statute miles recommended.
10. **Respect privacy.** No low passes over neighbors' yards to record.

### If Wisp ends up ≥ 0.55 lb (about 249.5 g or more) at takeoff

- **Register on FAADroneZone** as a "model aircraft" registration. Recreational flyers register as owners, with one number covering all their recreational drones (UNCONFIRMED this session; 48.100(b) needs only name, address and email).
  - The fee is $5 (48.30) [PRIMARY: 2015 rule table].
  - The certificate "expires 3 years after the date of issue unless it is renewed" (48.110(c), govinfo 2019 CFR) [PRIMARY].
- **Mark the registration number on the outside** of every aircraft (48.200 / 48.205; exact text not read, UNCONFIRMED). Carry proof of registration.
- **Comply with Remote ID**, using one of:
  - a standard Remote ID airframe, which a home-built drone will not be;
  - an FAA-accepted broadcast module, which must broadcast from takeoff to shutdown, keeping the drone in VLOS at all times (§89.115(a));
  - flying only inside a FRIA (§89.115(b)).
  - The module's weight usually decides the matter, since adding one generally means the drone needs it.
- **Everything else stays the same:** TRUST, CBO guidelines, VLOS, LAANC, and the 400 ft limit.
