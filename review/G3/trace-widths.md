# High-current copper (G3)

Generated by `tools/pcb/currents.py`. **Method:** the IPC-2221 conductor formula `I = k * dT^0.44 * A^0.725` (A in square mils; k = 0.048 for outer layers, 0.024 for inner layers). IPC-2152 replaced this with charts that usually allow narrower internal conductors; the charts are not reproduced here, so these numbers are a **conservative bound**, not an IPC-2152 result. Copper assumed: flight controller (6 layers, D-062) 1 oz (35 um) outer and 0.5 oz (17.5 um) inner; ESC 2 oz (70 um) outer and 1 oz inner, as the G1 mass budget assumes. Both are UNCONFIRMED until the G4 stackup is chosen; ordering the ESC in 1 oz would roughly double the ESC widths below.

Worked example (first row, 20 C, outer layer): A = (I / (k dT^0.44))^(1/0.725); the four layers share the current, so the solver finds the one width w at which the four cross-sections together carry 30 A.

What the layout does about it is listed in `review/G3/README.md` (copper actually drawn per path, measured from the board files). **The 30 A battery path is the weak point.** The widths above are for a long conductor in still air. The flight-controller path is not long: the battery pigtail pads, the shunt and the ESC wire pads sit side by side, and the copper between them is a solid island on L1, L3, L4 and L6 joined by stitching vias, wider than the IPC-2221 bound only where the parts leave room. Whether that is cool enough is UNCONFIRMED: the temperature rise must be measured at G6 (bench, props off, electronic load, thermocouple on the shunt) before any full-throttle flight.
